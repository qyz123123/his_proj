### Google: The Company That Made Computers the Doorway to Information

By *Yunzhe Qu*

A personal computer becomes much more valuable when it connects to information.

Without the internet, a PC can still write documents, run programs, and store files. But with the internet, the same machine becomes a library, map, newspaper, classroom, shop, and communication center.

BUT, **THE INTERNET WAS TOO LARGE TO USE WITHOUT SEARCH**

Google was founded in 1998 by Larry Page and Sergey Brin. Its early breakthrough was PageRank, a system that helped rank web pages by importance. The result was simple for users: type a question, receive useful answers.

That simplicity was historically powerful.

Google’s homepage was basically a search box. No complicated menu. No technical command. Just type. For ordinary people, this made the computer feel less intimidating. **The PC became a machine you could ask.**

Google also made online services feel normal. Gmail, Maps, Chrome, Docs, and YouTube gave users more reasons to open a computer every day. The value of the personal computer grew because the web became easier to navigate and more useful.

This connects to personal computer adoption even though Google arrived after PCs already existed. Google increased the daily usefulness of owning a computer. A family computer was no longer only for typing papers or playing games. It became the fastest path to information, directions, videos, email, school research, and public life.

For the driving question, Google shows that adoption depends on usefulness.

People keep using personal computers when those computers help them answer real questions.

Google made the computer feel like a gateway to the world.
