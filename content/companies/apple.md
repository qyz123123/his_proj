### Apple: The Company That Made Personal Computers Feel Personal

By *Yunzhe Qu*

Early computers were powerful, but they often looked like machines for experts.

If a computer feels cold, confusing, and technical, ordinary people may respect it, but they will not necessarily invite it into their homes.

BUT, **APPLE MADE COMPUTERS FEEL HUMAN**

Apple was founded in 1976 by Steve Jobs, Steve Wozniak, and Ronald Wayne. The Apple II, released in 1977, was one of the first highly successful personal computers. It had a keyboard, color graphics, expansion slots, and software that gave people practical reasons to use it.

This mattered because adoption is partly emotional. People needed to believe that a computer belonged in a classroom, a bedroom, or a small business office. Apple helped create that belief.

The Macintosh in 1984 pushed the idea further. It used a graphical interface, icons, windows, and a mouse. A user did not need to memorize every command. They could point, click, drag, and see what they were doing. **The screen became a visual workspace.**

Apple also understood design. The computer was not only a tool; it could be beautiful, friendly, and creative. That idea helped change the public image of computing from cold calculation to personal expression.

Later products like the iMac and MacBook Air continued this pattern. Apple made computers look less like industrial equipment and more like objects people wanted to live with.

For the driving question, Apple shows that U.S. technology companies contributed to PC adoption by reducing fear and increasing desire.

Apple did not just sell computers.

Apple sold the idea that computers could belong to ordinary people.
