### Mouse: The Small Device That Made Computers Pointable

By *Yunzhe Qu*

A keyboard can give commands.

But commands must be remembered.

For many early computer users, this was the problem. If you did not know what to type, the computer felt like a wall. It had power, but the power was hidden behind language.

BUT, **THE MOUSE MADE THE COMPUTER POINTABLE**

Douglas Engelbart demonstrated the mouse in 1968 during the famous “Mother of All Demos.” The device was small, but the idea was huge. Instead of only typing instructions, users could point at things on the screen.

That changed the relationship between human and machine.

With a mouse, a user could click an icon, open a menu, select text, move a window, draw a shape, or drag a file. These actions made the computer feel physical. **The screen became a place where people could touch ideas indirectly.**

The mouse became especially important with graphical interfaces such as the Macintosh and Microsoft Windows. It helped ordinary users understand computers without memorizing long command lists.

This was essential for adoption. A personal computer cannot spread widely if it feels like a machine only trained specialists can control.

The mouse lowered the barrier.

Children could click. Office workers could select. Designers could draw. Students could explore.

For the driving question, the mouse shows that personal computer adoption was not only about faster processors or lower prices.

It was also about interaction.

The mouse made the computer feel controllable.
