### Microsoft: The Company That Made the PC Useful Every Day

By *Yunzhe Qu*

A personal computer without useful software is just an expensive box.

It may have a processor, memory, and a screen, but ordinary people do not buy computers to admire hardware. They buy computers to write, calculate, learn, play, organize, and communicate.

This is where **MICROSOFT BECAME IMPORTANT**.

Microsoft began in 1975, just as microcomputers were moving from hobbyist experiments toward real products. Its early BASIC software helped people program small machines, but its larger historical role came through operating systems and applications.

MS-DOS became important after IBM released its personal computer in 1981. It gave many PC users a common software environment. That mattered because adoption requires trust. If every computer works differently, buyers hesitate. If many computers share the same system, schools, offices, software developers, and families can learn one pattern and reuse it.

Then Windows made the PC easier to approach.

Before graphical interfaces, users often had to type commands. If you forgot the command, the computer felt closed. Windows used icons, menus, windows, and the mouse to make the machine more visible. **The computer became less like a locked machine and more like a desk with tools on it.**

Microsoft Office made the PC even more necessary. Word, Excel, and PowerPoint turned personal computers into school machines, office machines, and home productivity machines.

For the driving question, Microsoft shows that personal computers spread not only because hardware improved, but because software gave people reasons to use them every day.

Microsoft made the PC feel practical.
